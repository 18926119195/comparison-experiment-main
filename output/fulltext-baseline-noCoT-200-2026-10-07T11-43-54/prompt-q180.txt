[Silver Jubilee Bridge]
0. The Silver Jubilee Bridge, "Widnes-Runcorn Bridge" or Runcorn Bridge crosses the River Mersey and the Manchester Ship Canal at Runcorn Gap between Runcorn and Widnes in Halton, England.
1.  It is a through arch bridge with a main arch span of 361 yards (330 m).
2.  It was opened in 1961 as a replacement for the Widnes-Runcorn Transporter Bridge, and was initially known simply as the Runcorn Bridge or Runcorn–Widnes Bridge.
3.  In 1975–77 it was widened, after which it was given its official name in honour of the Queen's Silver Jubilee.
4.  It carries the A533 road and a cantilevered footway.
5.  The bridge is recorded in the National Heritage List for England as a designated Grade II listed building.

[River Mersey]
0. The River Mersey is a river in the North West of England.
1.  Its name is derived from the Anglo-Saxon language and translates as "boundary river".
2.  The river may have been the border between the ancient kingdoms of Mercia and Northumbria and for centuries it formed part of the boundary between the historic counties of Lancashire and Cheshire.

[Deschutes River (Washington)]

[Mersey Gateway]
0. The Mersey Gateway is a multi-million pound transport scheme between Runcorn and Widnes in Cheshire, England.
1.  The main part of the project is the construction of the "Mersey Gateway" toll bridge that will span the River Mersey and the Manchester Ship Canal.
2.  The crossing, which will have three traffic lanes in each direction, will be located approximately 1.5 km to the east of the existing Silver Jubilee Bridge.
3.  Apart from the new bridge, the project also includes major civil engineering work realigning the existing road network, constructing new interchanges and landscaping 9 km of highway.

[Listed buildings in Widnes]

[Lac qui Parle River]
